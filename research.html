Does the strategy actually have an edge?

I traded a Bollinger Band and StochRSI setup by hand, then built a bot to run it. The bot looked profitable. This page is how I checked whether it really was, what I found, and what I changed because of it.

Three years of 1-minute OKX data · SOL, BTC, ETH, XRP · 100 USD per position · 0.05% fee per side

Short answer: the original rules had no edge after fees. Entering further beyond the band does: on SOL a whole region of settings stays profitable in both the training and the test period. Tested unchanged on coins it was never tuned on, the simplest version holds up best.

The process

  1. June 2026Live paper bot A FastAPI bot on Railway trading BTC, ETH, SOL and XRP on OKX prices with my manual rules: enter 1% beyond the Bollinger Band with StochRSI confirming, take profit 1%, stop loss 3%. After a few months it showed an 84% win rate and a profit.
  2. Market data cache OHLCV from OKX through ccxt, stored in SQLite, downloading only missing ranges. About 1.6 million 1-minute candles per coin.
  3. First backtester 15-minute candles, fees, a random-entry benchmark, and 1-minute data to decide which of TP or SL was hit first inside a candle.
  4. Reproducing the live bot The bot computes indicators on the candle that is still open, so a closed-candle backtest is not the same strategy. I rebuilt that behaviour on 1-minute data. It matched 26 of 29 live trades, 25 with the same outcome.
  5. Grid search 114 thousand combinations of band distance, oscillator filter, TP, SL and entry splits, with a simulator that jumps from event to event instead of walking every minute.
  6. Averaging into positions How I actually traded by hand: a second or third entry further away, TP from the average price, SL only after the last entry. 147 thousand two-entry settings plus a three-entry grid.
  7. Diagnostics When does it lose? Drawdowns, losing streaks, market regimes, and a Bollinger width filter.
  8. Volatility-scaled TP and SL Targets that grow and shrink with recent volatility instead of fixed percentages.
  9. Out-of-sample on other coins Settings frozen on SOL, then run unchanged on BTC, ETH and XRP, where all three years are unseen data.
  10. November 2026Backtest for everyone The simulator now runs in the browser on a whole portfolio of coins at once, so any setting can be checked on every market together. The live bot stays on the original rules as a working example.

Method

  • Train and test. Parameters are chosen only on November 2023 – November 2025. The last year is used once, to check.
  • No look-ahead. Indicators use closed candles plus the current price, exactly as the live bot sees them. When TP and SL fall in the same minute and it is still ambiguous, the stop loss counts.
  • Random-entry benchmark. Every setting is compared with random entries under the same exit rules. With TP 1% and SL 2% a coin flip already wins about two thirds of the time, so a win rate alone proves nothing.
  • Selection rule. Profit divided by maximum drawdown on the training period, taking the centre of a stable region of good settings rather than the single best row.

Findings

  1. The original rules had no edge. Simulated exactly like the live bot over three years they won 76.8% of trades, while 77.5% was needed to break even after fees. The live log looked good because it ignored fees and because the polling loop missed fast wicks that a real stop order would have caught.
  2. Distance from the band is what matters. Entries closer than about 1.2% beyond the band never beat random. Between 1.7% and 2.2%, 73–91% of all TP and SL combinations were profitable in both train and test on SOL.
  3. Win rate is misleading. The median two-entry setting won 92% of its trades and still lost money.
  4. Most of the capital belongs on the first entry. Results improve steadily as more goes to the signal entry. Doubling down does worst, and three entries overfit: 98% wins on train, losses on test.
  5. Lowering the TP after averaging hurts. The win rate barely moves, but the large wins that pay for the losses disappear.
  6. Narrow bands only matter for close entries. At 1.5–1.9% beyond the band the distance already filters them out.
  7. Scaling with volatility fixed the bad year on SOL, but did not help on every coin.
  8. The best SOL setting did not transfer; the simplest one did.

The live bot today

The bot on the Live page keeps running the original rules on BTC, ETH, SOL and XRP. It is there to show how the bot works end to end, not to test new ideas: new settings are checked in the backtest below, on years of data instead of weeks.

  • Fees in the log. Every trade is stored gross and net. Old trades get an estimated fee.
  • Lighter on the exchange. One price request for all coins every 2 seconds; 15-minute candles are fetched only when a new one closes.
  • Separate data branch. History and open positions are written to a bot-data branch on GitHub, so saving trades does not trigger a redeploy.

Limitations and next steps

  • Funding fees on perpetual futures and slippage on stop orders are not modelled.
  • Roughly 40–280 trades per coin in three years: encouraging, not proof.
  • Next: Monte Carlo resampling of trades for drawdown and ruin probability, and order execution on an exchange testnet compared with the backtest.
